# Workers' Comp Class Codes for Solar, HVAC & Electrical Crews, Explained

Workers' compensation for energy contractors isn't a single code. Solar installation, HVAC service, and electrical work each carry distinct injury patterns — and carriers rate them differently. Getting the classification wrong means either overpaying on premium or getting hit with a surprise bill at audit.

## Why Class Codes Matter for Energy Contractors

Every job function on your crew maps to a class code that reflects its hazard level. Roof-based solar installation carries different risk than electrical panel work, which carries different risk than office and admin staff. A single blanket "contractor" code either overrates your low-hazard staff or underrates your high-hazard crews — and an audit will catch the mismatch.

## The Injury Patterns That Drive Rating

- **Roof falls and ladder/lift injuries.** Solar installation is roof-based work by nature. This is the single biggest driver of solar-crew workers' comp exposure.
- **Electrical shock and arc-flash.** Electrical contractors and HVAC techs working on live systems carry meaningful shock and burn exposure.
- **Confined-space and lifting injuries.** HVAC service work often means crawlspaces, attics, and mechanical rooms — awkward lifting and confined postures.
- **Vehicle and equipment incidents.** Techs driving between multiple jobsites daily add auto-adjacent exposure that factors into overall program design, even though it's rated separately under commercial auto.

## Common Classification Mistakes

- **Lumping installers and office staff under one code.** Office and admin staff should be classified separately at a much lower rate.
- **Using a general "electrician" or "HVAC" code without splitting installation vs. service work**, when your state's rating bureau distinguishes them.
- **Treating 1099 subcontractors as excluded** without properly documenting that they carry their own coverage — a gap that surfaces expensively during an audit or after a claim.
