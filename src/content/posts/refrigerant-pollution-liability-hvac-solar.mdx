# Does General Liability Cover a Refrigerant Release? (No — Here's What Does)

Almost every standard general liability policy contains a pollution exclusion. It's boilerplate language most contractors never read closely — until a refrigerant release, a fuel spill, or an equipment-disposal issue turns into a claim that GL simply won't touch.

## Where Energy Contractors Actually Carry Pollution Exposure

- **Refrigerant handling.** HVAC service and installation work involves refrigerants that are tightly regulated and, if released, can trigger both environmental liability and regulatory exposure.
- **Fuel and chemical spills.** Generators, job vehicles, and stored fuel on a job site or in the shop carry spill exposure that standard GL was never built to cover.
- **Equipment and panel disposal.** Decommissioning old solar panels, batteries, or HVAC units can carry liability tied to how that material is handled and disposed of.

## Why the GL Pollution Exclusion Exists

General liability is priced and underwritten around bodily injury and property damage from normal operations — not the specialized, often regulatory, exposure of a chemical or refrigerant release. Carriers carve pollution out of GL specifically so they can price it separately, through a dedicated pollution liability policy, based on your actual exposure.

## What Pollution & Environmental Liability Actually Covers

- Accidental refrigerant release during HVAC service or installation
- Fuel, oil, and chemical spills from job vehicles and generators
- Third-party bodily injury and property damage from a release
- Cleanup and remediation costs
- Defense costs for environmental claims — which can be significant even when the underlying claim is small

## Sudden vs. Gradual Release Coverage

Pollution policies typically distinguish between **sudden** releases (an abrupt spill or leak) and **gradual** releases (a slow leak over time). Most energy contractors are primarily exposed to sudden-release scenarios, but it's worth confirming which your policy actually covers — a basic form that only covers "sudden and accidental" releases can leave a slow refrigerant leak uncovered.

## The Bottom Line

If your crew handles refrigerant, runs fuel-powered equipment, or disposes of panels and batteries, a standard GL policy almost certainly excludes the claim that matters most.
